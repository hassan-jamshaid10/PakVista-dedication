Hunza Valley

Nestled in the Gilgit-Baltistan region of northern Pakistan, Hunza Valley is renowned for its towering snow-capped peaks, ancient forts, and turquoise lakes. Surrounded by the Karakoram mountain range, Hunza is one of the most visited destinations in Pakistan, offering a blend of breathtaking natural scenery, centuries-old culture, and warm hospitality. The valley is often described as a paradise on earth and attracts trekkers, photographers, and travelers from around the globe every year.

Top Attractions

Rakaposhi Viewpoint

The Rakaposhi Viewpoint is one of the most popular stops along the Karakoram Highway. From here, visitors get an unobstructed view of Rakaposhi, a mountain that stands at over 7,700 meters and is famous for having one of the greatest continuous vertical reliefs in the world. The viewpoint is equipped with a small rest area where tourists can enjoy tea while taking in the scenery, making it a favorite photography spot for travelers passing through the valley.

Attabad Lake

Attabad Lake was formed in January 2010 when a massive landslide blocked the Hunza River, submerging the surrounding area and creating a lake with vivid turquoise-blue waters. Today, it is one of the most photographed locations in Hunza. Visitors can enjoy boating across the lake, surrounded by dramatic mountain cliffs on either side, and several lakeside restaurants and resorts have since developed along its banks.

Baltit Fort

Perched above the town of Karimabad, Baltit Fort is over 700 years old and served as the residence of the rulers (Mirs) of Hunza for centuries. The fort showcases a unique blend of Tibetan and Ladakhi architectural influences and has been carefully restored with support from the Aga Khan Trust for Culture. From its elevated position, visitors can enjoy sweeping views of the entire Hunza Valley, making it both a historical landmark and a scenic destination.
